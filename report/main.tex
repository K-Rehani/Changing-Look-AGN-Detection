\begin{abstract}
A changing-look active galactic nucleus (AGN) can show a striking change in its optical continuum and broad emission-line spectrum between observations. In summer 2025 I explored the possibility of finding such objects by comparing repeat Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS) quasar spectra. The surviving notes contain catalog-selection steps, exploratory difference plots, and a trial area cut. Several original spectrum plots are embedded in those notes and reproduced here as historical illustrations. The original code, intermediate arrays, exact downloaded spectra, and object-level measurements are unavailable, so the plots cannot be reanalysed as confirmed candidates. I subsequently wrote a new Python package to make the repeat-spectrum idea reproducible. It selects pairs, reads FITS spectra and quality information, measures selected lines with local continua and formal errors, fits simple broad Balmer components, and prepares review plots. A synthetic example and eight tests check parts of the implementation. This report explains the physics and numerical choices, separates the 2025 exploration from the later code, and describes what is still needed to validate candidates on real data.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question and physical background}
The practical question is whether two optical spectra of the same AGN show a change that deserves closer examination as a possible changing-look event. An AGN spectrum combines a continuum associated with accretion, stellar light from the host galaxy, broad emission from gas with large line-of-sight velocities, narrow emission from more extended gas, and sometimes absorption or blended features. H$\beta$ lies near 4861~\AA, H$\alpha$ near 6563~\AA, and the usually narrow [O\,III] doublet near 4959 and 5007~\AA. Broad and narrow components overlap, so a change in the total height of a line need not be a change in the broad-line region.

Broad-line appearance or disappearance is a useful optical signature of a changing-state AGN. Ordinary continuum variability, relative flux-calibration differences, changing amounts of host light inside the fiber, sky-subtraction residuals, and incomplete wavelength coverage can imitate aspects of a transition. Variable obscuration can also change the observed spectrum. Optical spectra alone may not decide between changing accretion state and changing obscuration; temporal, photometric, and multiwavelength information help \cite{ricci2023,guo2024}. The code developed here therefore produces \emph{review priorities}, not definitive physical classifications.

The project began with an interest in line identification and AGN spectral types. A 2025 presentation discussed FWHM, equivalent width, continuum fitting, and types 1, 1.5, 1.8, 1.9, and 2. Those slides show a direction being considered, not a completed general line finder or type classifier. The project-updates notes then shifted to repeat SDSS quasar spectra and direct comparisons. That narrower task is the scope of the later implementation.

The early comparison subtracted the fluxes and investigated an area under the difference spectrum. The notes first discuss the whole range, then a rest-frame 4600--5000~\AA{} interval near H$\beta$, with an exploratory cut of 1000. They describe taking an absolute value \emph{after} integrating the signed difference. This is not generally the same as integrating the absolute difference:
\begin{equation}
 A_{\rm signed}=\left|\int_W[f_2(\lambda)-f_1(\lambda)]\,d\lambda\right|,
 \qquad
 A_{\rm absolute}=\int_W|f_2(\lambda)-f_1(\lambda)|\,d\lambda .
\label{eq:areas}
\end{equation}
Positive and negative changes can cancel in the first quantity. Both have integrated-flux units if the flux-density and wavelength conventions are fixed. Neither has a universal threshold without a specified window, grid, noise model, normalization, and empirical validation. The value 1000 was a trial plotting cut, not an established classifier.

\section{The later reproducible implementation}
\subsection{Inputs and repeat-observation manifest}
The new package expects the SDSS \texttt{DR16Q\_v4.fits} quasar-only catalog, documented by SDSS \cite{lyke2020,sdssdatamodel}. The \texttt{select-pairs} command reads $Z$, \texttt{NSPEC}, the primary plate/MJD/fiber, and the duplicate arrays. It requires finite $0<z<0.8$ by default and \texttt{NSPEC}$\geq1$, keeps only positive observation identifiers, removes exact duplicate triples, and chooses the earliest and latest distinct MJDs. It writes one CSV row per selected catalog object. The rest-frame separation is
\begin{equation}
\Delta t_{\rm rest}
=\frac{\mathrm{MJD}_{\rm late}-\mathrm{MJD}_{\rm early}}{1+z}.
\label{eq:time}
\end{equation}
This gives context for variability, but two epochs do not reveal when a transition occurred. Selecting a quasar-only catalog can miss objects classified differently in another epoch, so the input itself limits completeness.

The redshift cut helps retain H$\beta$ in optical coverage, but does not guarantee H$\alpha$. At $z=0.8$, H$\beta$ appears near $8750$~\AA, while H$\alpha$ would lie near $11800$~\AA. Actual usable coverage depends on the instrument and data quality; the code checks each line separately. The notes' rough safe redshift limits were working estimates, not universal spectrograph boundaries.

The optional \texttt{fetch} command uses \texttt{astroquery} to retrieve individual SDSS spectra by plate, MJD, fiber, and release \cite{astroquery}. A local cache avoids repeat retrieval. A log records the requested release, retrieval time in UTC, SHA-256 file hash, and errors. Survey spectra are not redistributed in the repository. A failed download remains a logged failure. The live SDSS retrieval path has not been demonstrated here on a full real-data sample.

\subsection{Wavelengths, fluxes, and usable pixels}
For a standard individual SDSS spectrum, the reader uses HDU 1 columns \texttt{loglam}, \texttt{flux}, and \texttt{ivar}. It forms
\begin{equation}
\lambda_{\rm obs}=10^{\texttt{loglam}}\ \text{\AA},
\qquad
\lambda_{\rm rest}=\frac{\lambda_{\rm obs}}{1+z}.
\label{eq:wavelength}
\end{equation}
The stored flux density remains in its observed-frame SDSS scale. Its conventional unit is
\[
10^{-17}\,\mathrm{erg\,s^{-1}\,cm^{-2}}\,\text{\AA}^{-1}.
\]
Dividing wavelength by $1+z$ does not turn the flux density into a rest-frame luminosity density. Rest-frame coordinates determine the line windows; observed-wavelength widths determine observed integrated line flux.

The reader rejects nonfinite flux, nonfinite or nonpositive inverse variance, and pixels with a nonzero \texttt{and\_mask} when that column is present. Selected \texttt{or\_mask} bits may also be rejected explicitly, but the default does not reject every OR bit. Bad pixels and gaps are especially important near a narrow line or sky residual. The implementation requires valid sidebands and line windows without large gaps. This is a starting rule; a real-candidate review should examine the SDSS reduction, wavelength solution, spectral resolution, sky residuals, and flux calibration.

\subsection{Continuum and integrated line flux}
A local continuum is fitted separately in each epoch for each line. If $x_i=\lambda_{{\rm rest},i}-\lambda_0$ and $f_i$ is the observed-frame flux density, the model in two sidebands is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}_i=a+b x_i .
\label{eq:cont}
\end{equation}
The coefficients minimize $\sum_{i\in C}\mathrm{ivar}_i(f_i-a-bx_i)^2$ over valid sideband pixels $C$. With design matrix $X$ and diagonal inverse-variance matrix $W$, the formal coefficient covariance is $(X^{\mathsf T}WX)^{-1}$. The implementation scales it upward when the sideband reduced $\chi^2$ exceeds one. This accounts approximately for scatter in the sidebands, but not correlated pixels, curvature, or calibration systematics.

For line pixels in window $L$, trapezoidal weights $u_i$ are calculated on that spectrum's own rest-wavelength samples. Since $d\lambda_{\rm obs}=(1+z)d\lambda_{\rm rest}$, observed line flux is
\begin{equation}
F_{\rm line}=\sum_{i\in L}(1+z)u_i[f_i-(a+bx_i)].
\label{eq:flux}
\end{equation}
In standard SDSS units the numerical result is in $10^{-17}\,\mathrm{erg\,s^{-1}\,cm^{-2}}$. Let $v_i=(1+z)u_i$ and $\mathbf{q}=(\sum_i v_i,\sum_i v_i x_i)^{\mathsf T}$. The formal variance implemented is
\begin{equation}
\sigma_F^2=\sum_{i\in L}\frac{v_i^2}{\mathrm{ivar}_i}
+\mathbf{q}^{\mathsf T}\operatorname{Cov}(a,b)\mathbf{q}.
\label{eq:variance}
\end{equation}
The terms represent line-pixel noise and fitted-continuum uncertainty, assuming independent pixels. For a positive fitted continuum at line center, rest equivalent width is
\begin{equation}
{\rm EW}_{\rm rest}=\frac{F_{\rm line}}{(1+z)a}.
\label{eq:ew}
\end{equation}
This is not a pure broad-component EW when narrow or blended features lie inside the window.

H$\alpha$'s broad integration window contains [N\,II] and narrow H$\alpha$; Mg\,II can blend with Fe\,II. The [O\,III] 5007 measurement is only a warning diagnostic: a sufficiently significant and substantial change may suggest calibration or aperture trouble, although genuine narrow-line variability is possible. It is not an automatic normalization anchor.

\subsection{Broad Balmer fits and review flag}
For H$\beta$ and H$\alpha$, the code compares a narrow-only model with a narrow-plus-broad model. Each has a linear continuum and a narrow Gaussian; the H$\alpha$ complex adds [N\,II] at 6548 and 6583~\AA{} with a tied width and shift and a fixed 6583-to-6548 amplitude ratio of 2.96. A positive broad Gaussian near the Balmer center is
\begin{equation}
g_b(\lambda)=A_b\exp\!\left[-\frac{(\lambda-\lambda_b)^2}{2\sigma_b^2}\right],
\qquad
\mathrm{FWHM}_v=\frac{2\sqrt{2\ln2}\,\sigma_b}{\lambda_0}\,c .
\label{eq:broad}
\end{equation}
Here $\sigma_b$ and $\lambda_0$ are in rest \AA{} and $c=299792.458\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$. The reported broad flux integrates the fitted Gaussian with a $(1+z)$ factor, because its amplitude remains an observed-frame flux density.

The fits use inverse-variance weighted least squares. With $n$ fitted pixels and $k$ parameters the code calculates $\mathrm{BIC}=\chi^2+k\ln n$ under an independent-Gaussian-error approximation. It provisionally detects a broad component when the fits converge, the broad model improves BIC by more than 10, formal broad-flux S/N is at least 5, FWHM is at least $1000\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$, and the width is below its ceiling. A pair is flagged for \emph{review} if provisional detection status changes and the fitted broad-flux change exceeds three combined formal standard deviations. An integrated-window change contributes to the separate priority score but does not alone set this flag.

These are heuristic cuts, not calibrated probabilities. The model omits host templates, Fe\,II, absorption, asymmetric profiles, detailed instrumental resolution, and epoch-to-epoch covariance. Calibration or differing host fractions can bias both continuum and broad fits. BIC and parameter covariance describe the adopted model, not the probability that a source is a changing-look AGN.

\subsection{Grid alignment and diagnostic plots}
The 2025 notes observed one pair whose wavelength arrays appeared to coincide and considered direct subtraction. This cannot be assumed for all pairs. The new measurements and fits use each epoch's native grid, so numerical ranking does not subtract unmatched pixels. Only the diagnostic plot interpolates both valid spectra onto a common overlapping rest-wavelength grid. It leaves gaps blank when neighboring source pixels are invalid. The plotted difference has no propagated covariance and is never used as a detection statistic.

\section{Checks and example output}
The tests use constructed spectra where the answer is known. A Gaussian line with a flat continuum has analytic integrated flux $A\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}$; the implementation recovers it to the test's numerical tolerance at $z=0$ and $z=0.6$ while preserving rest EW. A continuum-only offset is designed not to trigger a line-transition flag. Other checks cover different epoch grids, missing H$\alpha$ coverage, masked line pixels, zero inverse variance, earliest/latest catalog selection, and a FITS round-trip. Eight tests passed locally and in the repository's automated workflow for this version. These are implementation checks under simplified conditions, not measures of survey completeness.

The deterministic synthetic example has $z=0.15$ and slightly offset grids. Its earlier spectrum contains injected broad H$\beta$ and H$\alpha$ Gaussians; the later one retains continuum and narrow features but not the broad components. Gaussian noise is added. Figure~\ref{fig:synthetic} shows both epochs and a display-only difference. The code flags the pair for review; integrated-window changes are approximately $-27.1$ and $-24.1$ \emph{formal} standard deviations for H$\beta$ and H$\alpha$. Their size follows from a deliberately clear injected signal and the independent-noise model. This is not an SDSS detection or an estimate of false-positive rate.

\section{Interpretation, limitations, and next steps}
An interesting plot is only the beginning of candidate assessment. The original FITS files should be checked for adequate coverage and signal-to-noise in the continuum and line wings, redshift consistency, masks, sky residuals, resolution, and calibration. Both epochs should be examined separately with continuum and line models appropriate to the source. Narrow [O\,III] behavior may inform a relative-calibration check but is not an infallible standard. Further spectra and photometry can show whether the change persists or tracks the continuum. Multiwavelength data help assess obscuration and accretion interpretations \cite{ricci2023,guo2024}. A convincing broad-line transition can be proposed as a candidate after such review; a confirmed classification needs stronger evidence than a score crossing.

For this package to become a validated search, the next step is a controlled real-data run with catalog checksum and SDSS release recorded. Known changing-look examples and stable controls should be assembled independently. Visual review should sample low as well as high scores, not only apparent successes. Recovery and false-positive rates should be measured versus signal-to-noise, redshift, line coverage, flux state, and rest-frame time baseline. Thresholds should be tuned on one sample and evaluated on another. The historical area cut of 1000 must not be used as a validated boundary. No real-object candidate list, exhaustive line detection, classification completeness, or physical mechanism is established here.

\section{Conclusion}
The summer 2025 records show a progression from spectral-type ideas to repeat-quasar selection, difference plots, and exploratory visual filtering. Their embedded figures can be preserved, but the underlying observations and original code are missing, so they cannot establish changing-look classifications. The later package provides a narrower, testable workflow: it selects repeat DR16Q observations, measures selected lines with quality checks and formal uncertainties, fits simplified broad Balmer components, and produces review outputs. Synthetic checks support implementation behavior; real-survey validation remains the next scientific step.
